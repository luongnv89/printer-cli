## [Unreleased]

### Added
- `site/`: the examples site, a slideshow of every example in the README (#65). It is not published yet: deploying it is tracked in #68. Each slide shows one example: a sample page with the `prt` command that made it, or a terminal example with its output, and a prompt to give an AI agent for the same result, each with a copy button. It moves on every 7 seconds, waits while the mouse is over its text, while keyboard focus is on a control inside the slide (a copy button, the terminal sheet), while the credits are open, for 5 seconds after a touch or scroll in its text or its terminal sheet, and while the tab is hidden, and stops after any manual step (the arrows, ←/→, a slide's tick, a swipe) until Autoplay is pressed again (or Space); with reduced motion it starts stopped. The heading that takes focus after a manual step does not hold the show, so Space starts it again from there. The ticks are one tab stop: Tab reaches the current slide's tick, and ←/→, Home and End move along the row. The footer is one short line (the MIT licence, GitHub, PyPI) with a Credits panel that opens over the page for the NASA photo credit and the fonts' OFL licences, so the text keeps the height on a phone and nothing scrolls sideways at any width; Escape or a click outside closes it. On a touch screen of any width the buttons, the copy buttons and the ticks are 44px targets. Every sample image has alt text that says what the page shows, in the README too. Each slide's prompt asks for the sample shown, with the same files, titles and captions as its command. It uses the README's logo, only greys, black and white, and the system's light or dark theme. The folder is self-contained and MIT licensed (its own `LICENSE` copy): HTML, CSS, JavaScript, the sample images, the logo and two of the gazette's fonts with their licences, no build step and nothing loaded from elsewhere, so it can be served as it is. `tests/test_site.py` keeps the slides in step with the README, and the sdist leaves `site/` out.
- `prt bingo`: print bingo cards, a different one for each player (#37). `prt bingo --theme "road trip" -n 8` prints 8 cards, one per A4 page: the title in Caveat handwriting, a row of coloured B-I-N-G-O letters and a 5×5 grid of words round a free centre square, each word as large as fits its square. The words come from a bundled theme (`road trip`, the default, `beach`, `zoo`, `birthday` or `space`; the title is e.g. "Road Trip Bingo") or from a JSON words file written for the game (`--words-file words.json`, or `-` for stdin: `words` required, at least 25 and up to 200 different words of up to 24 characters; `title` and `free`, the centre square's text, optional), with which `--theme` can be any text and only names the game. Each card takes 24 of the words in a random order, and no two cards in a run hold the same set of words; a 25-word file, which makes only 25 different cards, is refused for more (exit code 3). `-n`/`--cards` is 1 to 30. The deal is random: the `-f json` line and the closing note give its `seed`, and `--seed N` deals the same cards again. A theme that is not bundled, with no words file, exits with code 2 and the new error kind `unknown_theme`; a bad words file exits with code 3 and names the word (e.g. `words[12] repeats words[3]`); nothing is written or printed. `--preview`/`-o` saves the cards without printing (one PDF, or one image per card), `--save` keeps a copy, and printing goes through the print guard with each card counted as a page and sent as its own job, so in agent mode more than 10 cards is refused whole (`page_cap_exceeded`). The `-f json` line gives the `title`, the bundled `theme`, the number of cards (`pages`), the `seed`, the number of `words`, the `paper`, the fit report (a title too long for two lines is cut with `…`) and the `estimate`. `prt styles` lists the `bingo` style and `prt schema bingo` prints the words file's JSON Schema. In code, `prt.bingo` holds the themes, the words file checks, `deal` and the card layout, and `prt.render.bingo_cards` the render step.
- `prt colour-in PHOTO`: turn a photo into a colouring page, black line art on white on A4. The photo is smoothed and cut into a few flat tones with Pillow's filters, and the outlines between the tones are drawn as black lines, so the shapes come out mostly closed and can be coloured in. The whole photo is printed, never cropped, centred in a frame that closes the shapes its edges cut through; the page is upright for a portrait photo and on its side for a landscape one. Every pixel is pure black or white, so the `-f json` `estimate` reports black and white. `--preview`/`-o` saves the page without printing and `--save` keeps a copy; agent mode, the page caps and the confirmation prompt apply as for every printing command, and `prt styles` lists it as the `colour-in` style (no fields, no input schema) (#36).
- `prt comic PHOTO…`: print 3 to 6 photos as the panels of a comic page on A4, with speech bubbles, thought bubbles and narration captions. The panels are laid out in rows in reading order (3 photos: one wide panel over two; 4: a 2×2 grid; 5: two, one wide, two; 6: three rows of two), each photo cropped to fill its panel inside a black border, under an optional handwritten title and byline. The words come from a JSON comic file (`--comic-file comic.json`, or `-` for stdin), one panel per photo: each panel takes an optional `caption` (a yellow narration box in its top-left corner, up to 100 characters) and up to 3 `bubbles`, each with its `text` (up to 140 characters), a `position` (`top-left`, `top`, `top-right`, `bottom-left`, `bottom` or `bottom-right`; by default the first bubble goes top-left, the second top-right and the third bottom-right), a `tail` pointing at the speaker (`left`, `center`, `right` or `none`) and a `kind` (`speech`, an oval with a pointed tail, or `thought`, a cloud with a trail of puffs); `title` and `byline` are optional, and `--title` and `--byline` override them. Without a comic file a built-in "The Big Day" comic is printed. Every bubble and caption is set in the bundled Caveat handwriting font as large as fits its space; text that still does not fit is cut with `…`, warned about, and reported in the `-f json` fit report (`fits`, `cut`) by where it is in the file, e.g. `"cut": {"panels[1].bubbles[0]": {"max_fit": 52, "given": 120}}`, so an agent can shorten it to `max_fit` characters and preview again. Fewer than 3 or more than 6 photos, or a comic file with another number of panels than photos, is a usage error (exit code 2); a bad comic file exits with code 3, its message saying where (e.g. `panels[1].bubbles[0].position must be one of …`), and nothing is printed. `--preview`/`-o` saves the page without printing, `--save` keeps a copy, and printing goes through the print guard (agent mode, page caps) as one A4 page. The `-f json` line gives the number of `photos`, the `title`, the `paper`, the fit report and the `estimate`. `prt styles` lists the `comic` style and `prt schema comic` prints the comic file's JSON Schema. In code, `prt.comic` holds the comic file checks, the panel grid and the bubbles, and `prt.render.comic_page` the render step.
- `prt disc PHOTO`: print a circular label straight onto a printable 12 cm CD or DVD through the printer's disc tray, for a mixtape, a wedding playlist or a game-night disc. The label is drawn on the 120 mm square round the disc at 300 dpi: the photo fills a printable ring 116 mm across with a 43 mm hole, the title (on two lines when it is long) and a subtitle are written above the hub, the tracks run in two columns either side of it (the first half on the left) and a footer line sits at the foot. Every line is fitted to the width the ring has at its height, so no text reaches the hub or the edge, and everything outside the ring, the corners and the hub is left blank (but for a faint near-white haze from JPEG compression up to about 2 mm from the ring's edges), so no ink goes on the tray or the clear centre of the disc. The text comes from a JSON label file (`--disc-file disc.json`, or `-` for stdin: `title` required, `subtitle`, `tracks` and `footer` optional, `tracks` a list or one text with a newline between tracks, up to 24 of up to 80 characters) or from `--title`, `--subtitle`, `--footer` and `--track` (repeated, one track each, replacing the list), which override the file; without any, a built-in "Road Trip Mix" label is printed. The text is set in Caveat, off-white with a dark outline; text too long for its line is cut with `…` and reported in the `-f json` fit report (`fits`, `cut`). The label is sent with the disc size, the `disc` media source and the `disc` media type, and the confirmation prompt says to load a printable CD/DVD in the disc tray. A printer that lists its trays without a disc tray is refused before anything is sent (exit code 1, new error kind `disc_tray_unavailable`); agent mode still asks for `--yes` first. `--preview`/`-o` saves the label without printing (`.pdf` is a 120 mm square page), `--save` keeps a copy, and printing goes through the print guard (agent mode, page caps) as one page. The `-f json` line gives the `title`, the number of `tracks`, the `paper` and an `estimate` that counts only the printed ring. `prt styles` lists the `disc` style and `prt schema disc` prints the label file's JSON Schema. In code, `prt.disc` holds the label checks, the layout and `clip` (the ring mask), `prt.render.disc_label` the render step, and `prt.paper.DISC` the disc (`Paper` gains `load`, what the prompt tells the person to load when "… paper in the … tray" does not fit).
- `prt postcard PHOTO`: print a postcard on 4×6 photo paper from the photo tray, read on its side. The front is the photo in a thin white edge with an optional handwritten greeting across its foot; the back has "POSTCARD" over a centre divider, the message and a signature on the left, and a stamp box and four ruled address lines on the right. The text comes from a JSON postcard file (`--postcard-file postcard.json`, or `-` for stdin: `message` required, `greeting`, `signature` and `address` optional, the address one line per newline, up to 4) or from `--greeting`, `--message`, `--signature` and `--address` (repeated, one line each), which override the file; without any, a built-in postcard is printed. The text is set in Caveat; text too long for its space is cut with `…` and reported in the `-f json` fit report (`fits`, `cut`). Both sides print on one card: with `--duplex`, in one two-sided job flipped on the short edge, sent as a two-page PDF through the printer's CUPS queue (refused with exit code 1 and the new error kind `duplex_unavailable` when the printer does not report two-sided printing or has no CUPS queue); otherwise the card is turned over by hand. At a terminal prt prints the front, says how to put the card back (the blank side facing the way the photo side did, the same edge going in first) and prints the back, turned a half turn so it lines up, once you confirm. Without a terminal, and in agent mode, that is refused before anything is sent (exit code 2, new kind `manual_flip_required`), and `--side front` and `--side back` print one side per run. `--preview`/`-o` saves both sides without printing (one two-page PDF, or `NAME-front` and `NAME-back` images), `--save` keeps a copy the same way, and printing goes through the print guard with each side counted as a page. The `-f json` line gives the `method` (`duplex`, `flip`, `front` or `back`), the `jobs` by side, the `paper`, the `estimate` of the sides printed and, for a manual flip, the `flip` instructions. `prt styles` lists the `postcard` style and `prt schema postcard` prints the postcard file's JSON Schema. In code, `prt.postcard` holds the postcard checks, both layouts and `print_method` (which way the sides are printed), `prt.render.postcard_sides` the render step and `prt.render.send_two_sided` the two-sided job. `Paper` gains `duplex` (whether a printer's own duplexer may turn the sheet: A4 yes, 4×6 photo paper no), `PrintOptions` gains `sides` (an IPP `sides` keyword such as `two-sided-short-edge`, sent over IPP and to CUPS), the CUPS path now passes the media type, and `printing.queue_for` finds a printer's CUPS queue.
- `prt strip PHOTO PHOTO PHOTO PHOTO` and `prt instant PHOTO`: 4×6 photo prints from the printer's photo tray. `prt strip` lays four photos out in a column like a photo booth, with a handwritten caption at the foot, and prints the column twice side by side so the sheet cut along its dashed middle line gives two 2×6 in strips; `--mono` prints the photos in black and white. `prt instant` prints one photo cropped square in a wide white frame with a handwritten caption under it, Polaroid-style. `--caption` sets the caption (60 characters on the strip, 80 on the instant photo); without it today's date is written, and `--caption ""` leaves it blank. Captions are set in Caveat, a handwriting font now bundled under the SIL Open Font License 1.1; text the font cannot print (emoji, other scripts) is refused with exit code 2, and a caption too long for its space is cut with `…` and reported in the `-f json` fit report (`fits`, `cut`). Another number of photos than four is a usage error. `--preview`/`-o` (a `.pdf` is a 4×6 in page) and `--save` work as in `prt booth`, and printing goes through the print guard (agent mode, page caps). `prt styles` lists the `strip` and `instant` styles. In code, `prt.photocard` holds the layouts and `prt.render.strip_page` / `instant_page` the render steps.
- `prt recipe URL|FILE`: print a recipe as 6×4 in index cards on 4×6 photo paper from the photo tray: the title in handwriting over a red rule, the ingredients and the numbered steps on blue lines in two columns. From a web page the recipe is read from its schema.org `Recipe` JSON-LD (name, ingredients, steps, yield, preparation and cooking times), else from the lists under its "Ingredients" and "Instructions"/"Method"/"Directions"/"Steps" headings, with the article extractor; links are fetched as `prt article` fetches them. A recipe JSON file (`prt schema recipe`), a saved HTML page, a plain-text recipe or `-` (stdin) also work. The type is as large as lets the recipe fit on one card; a longer recipe runs on to more cards, up to 6, and every ingredient and step is printed; a title too long for two lines is cut with `…` and reported in the `-f json` fit report (`fits`, `cut`). A page or file with no recipe exits with code 3 and the new error kind `no_recipe`, and nothing is printed. `--preview`, `--save`, `--yes`, the print guard and the `-f json` line with its `estimate` work as in `prt article`. In code, `prt.recipe` reads and lays out the recipe and `prt.render.recipe_cards` is the render step.
- `prt.paper`: the sheet a page is laid out on. A `Paper` gives the page's pixel size at its resolution, the media size, input tray and media type it is printed with, and its area. `render.send_pages` takes a `paper` (A4 by default, sent exactly as before) and submits each page with that media, tray and media type, so the 4×6 commands print from the photo tray on photo paper; `PrintOptions` and the IPP driver gain `media_type` (sent as `media-col`'s `media-type`). `guard.estimate` takes a `paper` too: a cartridge's yield is rated in A4 pages, so the ink of a smaller sheet counts by its area and the summary names the size, e.g. `"~1 page (4×6), colour, est. ink 1%"`. `prt styles -f json` gives each style's `paper` (`size`, `media`, `tray`). A later size or tray (A6 postcards, printable discs) is one more `Paper`.
- `prt card PHOTO`: print a greeting card. The photo and a verse go on one A4 sheet, single-sided, as four A6 panels laid out to fold into a card with every panel the right way up: the front (the photo in a gold frame and the greeting), the inside left (an optional message, else blank to write on), the inside right (the verse and a signature) and the back (a small line). The inside panels are printed upside down on the top half of the sheet, and short marks at the sheet's edges show the folds: fold in half from top to bottom with the printed side out, then in half again with the photo on the front. The text comes from a JSON card file (`--card-file card.json`, or `--card-file -` for stdin: `greeting` and `verse` required, `message`, `signature` and `back` optional) or from `--greeting`, `--verse`, `--message`, `--signature` and `--back`, which override the file; without any, a built-in "Best Wishes" card is printed. In the verse a newline starts a new line and a blank line a new stanza. `--preview`/`-o` saves the sheet without printing, `--save` keeps a copy, and printing goes through the print guard (agent mode, page caps). With `-f json` the output carries the fit report (`fits`, `cut`), the `estimate` and the folding instructions (`fold`). `prt styles` lists the `card` style and `prt schema card` prints the card file's JSON Schema. A new agent skill, `skills/greeting-card/SKILL.md`, has the agent write a verse for the photo and the occasion, preview the card and print it. In code, `prt.card` holds the card file checks, the panels and the imposition (`impose`), and `prt.render.card_page` the render step.
- Four more `prt booth` styles, made from the same photo and story file: `--style certificate` (an award certificate: ivory paper in a green-and-gold border, the title, the photo in an oval gold frame, the name, the award, a citation, a date line with today's date, a signature line and a gold seal), `--style sports` (a sports trading card: royal-blue card with stripes and cut marks, the team, a full-width photo with a star badge, the name on a slanted red bar, the position and a stats box), `--style missing` (a missing-pet poster: huge red MISSING, "Have you seen this cat?", the photo, the name, a reward line, a description and tear-off tabs that each carry the contact) and `--style movie` (a movie poster: tagline, the photo fading into a dark poster, the title in large gold capitals, stars, a release line, the credits block and a rating box). Each reads the story fields in its own way (`prt styles -f json` describes every field on every style), has its own built-in text when no story is given, reports text cut to fit as the other styles do, and has a sample page in `assets/samples/`. `--preview` saves the page without printing. The MCP `preview_booth` tool, `prt styles` and the photo-booth skill list them too.
- `skills/letter-gazette/SKILL.md`: an agent skill that uses print and scan together. It scans a handwritten letter with `prt scan --format jpeg`, has the agent transcribe it (word for word, with `[illegible]` for what it cannot read, and asking before printing personal details), writes the gazette story file with the transcription as the story, previews the edition and prints it with `prt gazette`, the scan as the lead picture. `prt skills install` installs it, and the Claude Code plugin carries it.
- The demo printer's JPEG scan (`prt -p demo scan --format jpeg`) is now a readable image, a sample handwritten letter on a lined A4 sheet (greyscale with `--mode gray`), instead of an empty JPEG header that no command could open, so the letter-gazette workflow runs end to end with `-p demo`. The demo PDF scan is unchanged.
- `prt compose PAGE`: print a page described as JSON blocks, for printables no other command makes. Blocks: `heading` (three levels), `text` (paragraphs, three sizes), `image` (a local JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, BMP or TIFF file, with an optional height in mm and caption), `qr` (a QR code of up to 500 characters, sized in mm), `columns` (2 to 4 lists of blocks side by side) and `rule`; most take `align`. The blocks are laid out top to bottom on A4 and run on to more pages when one is full, up to 10; nothing is cut. `prt compose -` reads the page from stdin. `--preview`/`-o`, `--save`, `--yes` and the `-f json` line with its `estimate` work as in `prt article`, and printing goes through the print guard (agent mode, page caps). `prt schema page` prints the page file's JSON Schema, and the page file is checked against that same schema before anything is drawn, written or printed: an invalid page file exits with code 3 (kind `invalid_input`) and the message says where, e.g. `blocks[2].level must be one of 1, 2, 3, not 4`. Image paths are untrusted: only local regular files are read (a relative path from the page file's folder), web links are refused and nothing is downloaded, and each image may be at most 30 MiB and 50 megapixels, 20 per page file. Output names that would overwrite the page file or an image are refused with exit code 2. New dependency: `segno`, a pure-Python QR code encoder. In code, `prt.compose` holds the schema, the checker, image loading and the layout, and `prt.render.composed_pages` the render step.
- `prt skills list` and `prt skills install`: install the bundled agent skills (`photo-booth`, `article-print`) for Claude Code (`--claude`, the default: `~/.claude/skills`) or for agents that read `~/.agents/skills` (`--agents`), in your home folder or, with `--project`, in the current folder; `--dir PATH` installs into any skills folder, and skill names pick only some. An installed file that differs from the bundled one is never replaced without `--force`: the command then installs nothing and exits with code 1, kind `skill_exists`. `-f json` reports each skill as `installed`, `updated`, `unchanged` or `replaced`. The wheel now carries the skills (as `prt/bundled_skills`), so an installed `prt` has them; the repository's `skills/` folder stays the one copy.
- A Claude Code plugin: `.claude-plugin/plugin.json` makes the repository a plugin with the bundled skills and the `prt mcp` server (run with `PRT_AGENT=1`), and `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json` lists it, so `/plugin install printer-cli --marketplace luongnv89/printer-cli` installs it.
- `AGENTS.md` (using prt from an agent, and working on the repository) and `llms.txt` (an index of the docs for language models) at the repository root.
- `prt mcp`: printer-cli as an MCP server over stdio, for agents in any MCP client. Tools: `status`, `ink`, `list_styles`, `preview_booth`, `preview_article` and `print_file`. The previews print nothing and return the rendered page images in the tool result, with the fit report, the ink estimate and a single-use token (30 minutes) that `print_file` prints exactly. `print_file` prints a file only with `confirm: true` and refuses otherwise (kind `confirmation_required`; a bad token is `invalid_token`); every print goes through the print guard, so page caps and agent mode apply. Story and article text are passed as data (`story` object, article `content`). Errors are tool errors carrying the `-f json` error object. The MCP SDK is an optional extra, `pip install 'printer-cli[mcp]'`; without it `prt mcp` exits with code 2, kind `missing_dependency`. In code, `prt.render` holds the booth and article page steps the CLI and the server share, and `prt.mcp_server.Tools` the tools without the SDK.
- Agent guardrails. With `PRT_AGENT=1`, every command that prints or uses ink (`print`, `booth`, `article`, `gazette`, `maintain test-page`, `nozzle-check` and `clean`) refuses to run without `--yes`, even at a terminal (exit code 2, kind `agent_confirmation_required`). `prt print` gains `--yes` for this. `max_pages_per_run` and `max_pages_per_day` in the config file cap the pages one command and one day may print; in agent mode the per-run cap defaults to 10. A print over a cap is refused whole before anything is sent (kinds `page_cap_exceeded`, `daily_page_cap_exceeded`), and while a cap is in force a file whose pages cannot be counted is refused (`page_count_unknown`). Pages per day are kept in `~/.cache/prt/pages.json`; a page counts once its job was sent, even if sending failed. Caps also apply in the TUI. With `-f json`, `prt booth`, `prt article` and `prt gazette` add an `estimate` of the rendered pages: page count, colour use, ink use per colour as a share of a cartridge, and a `summary` such as `"~3 pages, colour, est. ink 4%"`. In code, `prt.guard` holds the checks (`check`, `reserve`, `authorize`), the page counting and the estimate, for other front ends to reuse.
- `prt styles` lists every booth, article and gazette style; with `-f json` each entry gives the command and the `args` that pick the style, what it prints, its fields with their character limits and whether they are required, how many photos it takes, its page limit, its input schema and a link to a sample page. `prt schema story`, `prt schema article` and `prt schema gazette` print the JSON Schema (draft 2020-12) of the booth story file, the article JSON file and the gazette story file; a file that passes the schema is accepted by the command. An unknown schema name exits with code 2. In code, `prt.catalog` holds the styles and schemas, built from the limits the commands check, and `catalog.register_schema` adds a schema.
- `prt booth --story-file -` and `prt gazette --story-file -` read the story JSON from stdin, and `prt article -` reads the article from stdin (JSON when it starts with `{`, HTML when it starts with `<`, else plain text), so an agent can pipe its text in without writing a temporary file. The same size limits (64 KiB for a story, 5 MiB for an article) and checks apply as for a file: bad input on stdin exits with code 3 and nothing is printed.
- With `-f json`, errors are printed as a JSON object on stderr, `{"error": {"code": 3, "kind": "fetch_failed", "message": "…", "hint": "…"}}`, where `code` is the exit code and `kind` a stable name scripts and agents can branch on (`unreachable`, `usage`, `fetch_failed`, `job_failed`, …; see the README). Usage errors, Ctrl-C and a status shown from the cache get one too. Without `-f json` error output is unchanged. In code, `PrinterError` takes a `kind`, which defaults from its class or exit code.
- With `-f json`, `prt booth` and `prt gazette` report text that did not fit: `"fits"` is false when a field was cut with `…`, and `"cut"` gives each cut field, in page order, with how many of its characters were printed and how many were given, e.g. `"cut": {"headline": {"max_fit": 68, "given": 92}}`, so an agent can shorten the text to fit and try again. When nothing is cut the report is `"fits": true, "cut": {}`. The stderr warning is unchanged. In code, `booth.compose()` and `vintage.compose_pages()` return the cut fields as a dict of `layout.Cut` instead of a list of names.
- `prt print --wait` and `prt jobs wait ID`: wait until a job has finished and report its final state; a job that was cancelled or aborted exits with code 1. `--wait-timeout` sets how long to wait. `--wait` refuses files that go through CUPS, whose job ids the printer does not know. The demo printer's jobs now move from pending to processing to completed.

### Fixed
- `prt print --media disc` (and any `disc_…` media name) now asks the printer for the disc's size, 120 × 120 mm, as printers report it. The size was read from the name `disc_12cm_18x120mm` as 18 × 120 mm, the hub and outer diameters taken for a width and a height.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-23

### Added
- `prt booth PHOTO`: photo booth that prints a photo as an A4 newspaper front page with a short news story. Two styles, `--style metro` (colour tabloid) and `--style daily` (black-and-white broadsheet); a built-in default story; your own story via `--story-file story.json` or `--headline`/`--story`/`--kicker`/`--caption`/`--byline`; `--preview FILE` to render without printing; a warning names any text cut to fit the page. Unreadable photos and bad story files are rejected before anything is sent to the printer.
- `prt booth --style wizard` (an ornate trading card with an arched gold portrait frame, emblem, name ribbon and lore box) and `--style wanted` (a weathered parchment WANTED poster with a framed photo, name and reward line). Both reuse the story fields (headline as the name, kicker as the top label or "dead or alive" line, caption as the subtitle or reward, story as the lore, byline as the footer) and print their own built-in text when no story is given.
- `skills/photo-booth/SKILL.md`: an agent skill that looks at the photo, writes a fitting story, previews it and prints it with `prt booth`.
- `prt booth --save FILE`: keep a digital copy of the page as a PDF (one A4 page), PNG or JPEG and still print it; repeat it for several files. Copies are written before the printer is contacted, so they need no reachable printer and survive a declined prompt; `-f json` adds a `saved` list. `--preview`/`-o` can also write PDF and PNG.
- `prt article URL|FILE`: print a blog post, news article or X post as A4 magazine pages. `--style magazine` (accent bar, bold headline, three columns) or `--style classic-magazine` (ivory page, centred single column, drop cap, fleurons). Keeps the title, author, site and full text over as many pages as it needs (up to 30), and leaves out navigation, ads and other clutter. X posts are read through X's public oEmbed service; saved article JSON, HTML or text files also work. `--preview`/`--save` write a multi-page PDF or one image per page. An article that cannot be fetched or converted exits with code 3 and nothing is printed.
- `skills/article-print/SKILL.md`: an agent skill that tries `prt article` on the link and, when the page needs a login or JavaScript, reads the article itself and prints it from a JSON file.
- `prt gazette PHOTO...`: print one or more photos as a vintage newspaper after luongnv.com/news: cream paper and one ink, a blackletter masthead, a small-caps dateline, a Playfair Display headline and Old Standard TT text in ruled columns, with the photos toned from ink to paper. The front page holds the story, the lead photo and two plates, and each further page six more plates, so no photo is left out. Up to 57 photos (10 pages); more are refused with exit code 3. Takes the booth story flags and `--story-file`, and `--preview`/`--save` like `prt article`. Unreadable photos and output names that would overwrite a photo are refused before anything is written or sent, and when a page fails after others were queued, their job ids are named. `--landscape` lays the pages out on A4 on its side (the lead photo beside the story, plates three abreast); saved pages stay landscape, and each page is turned upright only for the printer.
- `prt list`: the saved printers and the connection status of each one, the same as `prt printers list`.
- Bundled fonts for the gazette: UnifrakturMaguntia, Playfair Display and Old Standard TT, under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, in `src/prt/fonts` with their licence texts.

### Changed
- `prt discover` now saves each printer it finds under a short name (for example `xp-6000`), so the separate `prt printers add` step is no longer needed. Printers already saved, matched by host name or IP address, are left as they are; a name already in use gets a `-2`, `-3` suffix; scanner-only devices are not saved; the default printer is never set or changed. The config file is rewritten only when a printer was added. The output names the saved printers: an "added" line in the box, two extra fields (saved name, `added`) at the end of each tab-separated line, and `saved_as`/`added` keys on each `-f json` item.
- `prt booth --preview`/`-o` picks the file type from the extension (`.pdf`, `.png`, `.jpg`, `.jpeg`, any case). A file name without one of these extensions is now refused with exit code 2 before anything is written, instead of being saved as JPEG.
- `prt printers list` checks each printer's connection by default and shows it as online or offline; `--no-check` skips the check (it was opt-in with `--check`). The printers are checked in parallel, each within `--timeout` when given, else 3 seconds or the configured timeout if lower. A printer that cannot be reached, or fails in any other way, is still listed and marked offline. The `-f json` shape is unchanged: `reachable` is `online`/`offline`, or `null` with `--no-check`.
- The package licence is now `MIT AND OFL-1.1`, and the fonts' OFL texts are listed as licence files.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-22

First public release of printer-cli (command: `prt`), a CLI and full-screen TUI for network printers.

### Added
- `prt status`, `prt ink` and `prt overview`: printer state, alerts, ink levels, paper trays, waste pad, firmware, uptime and SNMP page count. `status --watch N` samples continuously.
- `prt discover` (mDNS) and `prt printers list/add/remove/default` to find and save printers under short names.
- `prt maintain nozzle-check | clean | test-page | identify`. Head cleaning is refused while ink is low or the waste pad is flagged (`--force` overrides); nozzle check, cleaning and the test page ask for confirmation first (`--yes` in scripts).
- `prt print` over IPP, with JPEG conversion for images and the local CUPS queue for PDF; `prt jobs` / `prt jobs cancel`.
- `prt scan` over eSCL/AirScan, to PDF or JPEG, including `-o -` for stdout.
- `prt tui`: keyboard-driven dashboard with ink tanks in cartridge colours, Maintain, Print, Scan and Jobs tabs, and switching between saved printers.
- Drivers: generic IPP Everywhere / AirPrint, Epson (ESC/P2 remote mode on port 9100 for maintenance), and a replay driver; `-p demo` runs every command against a captured Epson XP-6000.
- Global options `--format json`, `--no-color`, `-q`, `--timeout`, `--config`, `--verbose` protocol trace; tab-separated output when piped; shell completion.
- Last known status is cached and shown, marked as cached, when the printer is asleep.
- Configuration in `~/.config/prt/config.toml`, with `PRT_CONFIG`, `PRT_PRINTER` and `PRT_TIMEOUT` overrides.
- Multi-colour (tri-colour) cartridges are drawn as striped tanks; duplicate trays that differ only in margins are merged.
- Logo, MIT license, and a `printer-cli` command alias alongside `prt`.
